# 5 LCA Checklist

# 5.02

The CO2 Removal Supplier provides a life cycle assessment (LCA) for biochar activity including disaggregated information on the emissions arising at different stages. The system boundary is set cradle-to-grave and includes emissions from production and supply of the biomass, from biomass conversion to biochar, and from biochar distribution and use. (GL Ref. 3.1)

All process emissions have been calculated in the LCA using the cradle-to-grave approach. The following emissions have been accounted for: Harvesting of the wood, production of wood chips, transport to the production site, the manufacturing of the biochar on site, transportation of the biochar, and use of the biochar. Regarding the infrastructure, the reactors manufacturing, installation and disposal are considered as part of the operation, and included in the assessment. The system boundary of this study accords to the Puro earth requirements. Respective emissions revised according to system boundaries.

# 5.04

The default baseline emission scenario for the project activity feedstock is zero, which is a conservative assumption since it is not taking into account methane emissions derived from decay of manure or combustion of waste biomass. If a non-zero baseline presented, needs to be accepted by Puro.earth.

By capturing waste forest biomass of low economic value from sustainably managed forests and stabilizing the carbon through pyrolysis, Carbon Cycle’s biochar safely captures CO2 that would otherwise be released back into the atmosphere. The wood is classified as energy wood, unsuitable for the timber industry and so the alternative pathway for the feedstock would be decomposition in the forest or unabated combustion in biomass energy production.

6.03 CO2 Removal Supplier shall be able to demonstrate additionality, meaning that the project must convincingly demonstrate that the CO2 removals are a result of carbon finance. Even with substantial non-carbon finance support, projects can be additional if investment is required, risk is present, and/or human capital must be developed. To demonstrate additionality, CO2 removal Supplier must provide full project financials and counterfactual analysis based on Baselines that shall be project-specific, conservative and periodically updated. Suppliers must also show that the project is not required by existing laws, regulations, or other binding obligations. (GL Ref. 1.2.3)

Revenue from the sales of CORCs is an important part of Carbon Cycle’s business case for future investment and to scale up the production technology. Revenue has been re-invested in repairs, improvements, and expansion of the facility. Operation and production of biochar requires skill and knowledge which Carbon Cycle has had to develop. Sales of CORCs make up approximately 23% of the revenues from the biochar operation. The net value realized by Carbon Cycle from the sales of the biochar only, is not sufficient to cover the production cost, and ongoing maintenance or expansion of the biochar production facility. A large barrier for small biochar producing businesses, is getting the biochar to market. The use of biochar in agriculture, although not completely new, is very much in its infancy. It is therefore a challenge to convince farmers in the area to change farming practices and adopt new products such as biochar.

Infrastructure at the Carbon Cycle facility is a challenge. In Q4 2024, Carbon Cycle will commission a new, larger production system. The revenue from carbon removal certificates is an essential part of the business case for further investment. The revenue from carbon credit sales not only supports infrastructure expansion but supports Carbon Cycle in marketing their product to farmers. When considering purely financial factors, a certain amount of fixed and variable external costs will be incurred, such as the cost of carbon project developers and sales, materials and equipment, and training workers and end-users.

Carbon Cycle have undertaken considerable investments to biochar production while hoping to benefit from the carbon credit market and bring growth to the carbon capture marketplace. The existence of the carbon revenue reduces the price risk from biochar sales and helps offset rising costs of production. The limiting factor for Carbon Cycle’s growth is the sale of its biochar. The carbon revenue therefore enables Carbon Cycle to partially subside the price of biochar which can enable farmers who may not otherwise be able to afford it, to try it with the operations. The producer hopes that this strategy will be beneficial in the long run for its business and that it will create customer loyalty that increases the value of the business and provides a solid base for investment decisions in further biochar production capacity.

# 7.07

Euse = LCA emissions of the use of biochar, including distribution up to the point of final use (GL Ref 4.5)

The mass-weighted average distance travelled for biochar to customers is 373 km. Greenhouse gases emissions from biochar transport have been calculated using the Ecoinvent 3.10 lorry Euro VI transport dataset. Moreover, the big bags and paper bags delivered from the suppliers is accounted for. Emissions related to the transport from the packaging manufacturers amount 0.4 kg CO2e per unit. Total biochar transport emissions, including the biochar and packaging mass, are 47 kg CO2e per tonne of dry biochar. Notwithstanding, inconsistencies of transport distances and weighted average transport distance may arise from the fact that for several buyers who are practising drop shipping the company address is being referenced instead of the actual application sites. The biochar is sold to farms, soil amendment companies, manure manufacturers. The application emissions are related to the processes that each company has for application. These processes can vary significantly, from hand feeding to animals to spreading and mixing. As a proxy for the average application process, an emissions profile for spreading dry fertilizer has been selected from Ecoinvent 3.10. The biochar application emissions are estimated to be 5 kg CO2e per tonne of dry biochar.
