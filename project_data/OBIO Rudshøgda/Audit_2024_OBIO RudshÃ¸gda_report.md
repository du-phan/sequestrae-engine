# 6.01

Evidence of Production Facility eligibility under the general rules of Puro Standard. (GL Ref. 1.2.1)

The produced biochar is not used for energy purposes. Biochar produced during the period has been marketed and sold as soil amendment and animal feed. (51.1.1).

The feedstock consists exclusively of wood chips: (81.1.4.) Feedstock supply for the wood chips is well documented and PEFC sustainably certified. (51.1.2).

The facility is EBC-certified. (51.1.7) Compliance with comparable conditions is outlined in this report, and all process emissions have been accounted for: Harvesting of the wood, chipping, transport of wood chips to the production site, the manufacturing of the biochar on site, transportation of the biochar, and combusted within the reactor to maintain the carbonisation process. The following emissions have been calculated in the LCA using the cradle-to-grave approach.

Use of the biochar: (51.1.3). Pyrolysis gases are captured and combusted. The remaining gases are combusted and exhausted. The exhaust emissions have been analysed and quantified. (51.2.4).

The produced biochar has a high, stable carbon content. The lab analysis performed by Eurofins in August 2023 demonstrated an organic carbon capture of 3.31 kg CO2/kg: (55.3.3). The produced biochar has a content of 90.9% in the dry state, which corresponds to the gross H/Corg molar ratio of 0.29 (Eurofins, 2023), well below the 0.7 threshold: (51.1.6).

OBIO has implemented appropriate measures to ensure the safe storage and transport of the biochar, including quenching of the biochar: They can provide a material safety data sheet for transport and storage: (81.1.8).

The potential for methane emissions from stockpiling of wet feedstock onsite and at the supplier has been considered in accordance with the criteria of the EBC (2020). There are no emissions from feedstock storage. The potential for fugitive release of syngas from pyrolysis is considered negligible.

# 1. Calculation Checklist

# 1.1 LCA emissions from biochar manufacturing (GL Ref. 4.4)

Emissions from the production process of the biochar are limited to electricity for the whole site, diesel for forklift usage and process emissions. Moreover, allocated emissions arise from the infrastructure, the Biomacon reactor. In total, the allocated emissions of greenhouse gases related to the manufacturing of biochar amount to 23 kg CO2e per tonne of dry biochar.

# 1.2 LCA emissions of the use of biochar, including distribution up to the point of final use (GL Ref 4.5)

The bagged biochar is transported to the customer by truck. The volume-weighted average distance travelled for biochar to customers is calculated using the tkm transported, including the biochar and packaging mass, 568 km. Greenhouse gas emissions from biochar transport have been 66 kg CO2e per tonne of dry biochar. The biochar is mainly mixed with manure or compost for use as a soil improvement medium and with feed when used as animal feed. So, as a conservative assumption to assess application emissions, the Ecoinvent dataset "solid manure loading and spreading, by hydraulic loader and spreader" has been used, considering that mechanical means are used in both end-uses: The biochar application emissions are estimated to be 4 kg CO2e per tonne of dry biochar.

# 1.3 CORCs

For January 2023, the net carbon removal is 3.05 tonnes CO2e/t of dry biochar: CORCs: 1.013,9

# 1.4 Quantity of CORCs (in evidence)

Given the mass of 332 dry tonnes sold during the assessed period, the resulting total CO2e removed was 1013,9 t CO2. CORCs: 1013,9
