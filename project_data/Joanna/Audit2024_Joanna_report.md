# 1. INTRODUCTION

350Solutions was contracted to perform an audit and validation of the production facility as well as verification of carbon dioxide removal credit (CORC) on West Fraser’s (WF) Joanna Mill in Kinards, South Carolina. The facility produces Oriented Strand Board (OSB) and generates syngas by gasifying waste biomass to produce heat for steam production essential for OSB manufacturing. This process results in the continual production of biochar. To address biochar accumulation, West Fraser has partnered with a local waste management company and soil additive manufacturer to utilize the biochar in soil products.

350Solutions conducted an audit of the process, lifecycle CO2 emissions assessment (LCA), and other administrative details to verify compliance with the requirements of the Puro.Earth Standard General Rules v3.1 [1] and Biochar Methodology (Edition 2022 Version 3) [2]. The audit and verification began with a document review and followed with a site visit and detailed data audit on July 11th, 2024. Additional files were requested and reviewed by 350Solutions to clarify the findings of the initial investigation.

Moreover, Puro.earth is currently transitioning to use the 4.0 version of the Puro General Rules [3]. Although the West Fraser facility and output audit described in this report were conducted using version 3.1 of the General Rules, certain rules described in the updated version of the document (v4.0), such as the Ongoing Issuance Rights, are applicable to all projects currently registered or in the process of getting registered on the registry. Section 5 of this report aims to assess whether the Joanna Mill meets the Ongoing Issuance Rights requirements outlines in Appendix A of the updated Puro General Rules (v4.0).

Based on the activities above, 350Solutions issued an initial version of this report on 09/12/2024 highlighting non-compliance areas needing to be addressed by West Fraser to be eligible for CORC issuance under the Puro registry, following the guidance of the Puro Biochar Methodology (v3.0). Between 09/12/2024 and 10/04/2024, West Fraser has provided files to address the concerns raised during the audit period. As a result of the amendments made, the total volume of CORCs verified by 350Solutions was 5995 as opposed to the 6022 initially claimed by the supplier. A summary of the issues raised by 350Solutions, and mitigating actions taken by West Fraser is presented in Appendix 1: Log of previous audit issues and responses of this report.

# 2. TECHNOLOGY DESCRIPTION

WF has developed a slow pyrolysis system that uses woody biomass to generate heat for steam and electricity production. The process also produces biochar used for soil amendment. The biochar, a charcoal-like substance with high carbon content, is made from woody biomass at WF’s Joanna facility, which produces Oriented Strand Board (OSB).

The facility utilizes gasification of waste biomass—fuel chips from non-merchantable wood, bark, sawdust, shavings, and hog fuel—to generate syngas. The biomass feedstock at the Joanna facility comes from three sources:

- Purchased utility-size waste wood chips and grindings from harvested treetops and limbs, including Southern Yellow pine family species and various hardwoods. The chipping and grinding occur in the forest.
- Purchased waste wood, including bark, sawdust, and hog fuel from sister mills and forest thinning to support forest health. These materials arrive as moist chips.
- Waste bark from log peeling in the OSB board production at the Joanna facility.
- OSB boards unfit for sale produced at the Joanna facility.

Biomass waste from sister mills is trucked into Joanna and piled up onsite (see images in PDD). This waste along with rejected OSB boards is moved onto conveyors that bring the biomass into the kilns, by diesel diggers. Biomass from Joanna’s own log peeling is collected on site and moved onto conveyors that bring it to the kilns.

Purchased wood chips and grindings processed offsite from Sustainable Forestry Initiative (SFI)-audited landscapes, trucked into Joanna, piled onsite (see PDD images) and moved onto conveyors that bring the biomass into the kilns, by diesel diggers.

The energy system at the Joanna facility consists of three parallel systems (A-Train, B-Train, and C-Train) that convert feedstock to outputs using wood waste, with natural gas as an auxiliary fuel. Each system includes a rotary kiln gasifier, a secondary combustion chamber, a saturated steam boiler, a multicyclone, and an induced draft fan. C-Train also has a thermal oil heater. The exhaust from these systems is directed to an electrostatic precipitator (ESP) and then to a common stack. Syngas generated from the gasifiers is combusted, and the resulting heat is used in the steam boilers and thermal oil heater.

The gasification process continuously produces biochar, which has been accumulating on-site at West Fraser for over 24 years (Figure 1). With evolving permitting requirements, the company is prohibited from stockpiling biochar on its site. It is now seeking a more efficient way to manage this 'waste' byproduct from the OSB production process. The most cost-effective options are to either burn the biochar on-site or explore profitable offtake agreements for its use elsewhere. However, the potential to generate revenue through carbon credits, along with West Fraser's commitment to environmental stewardship, has driven them to seek alternative applications for biochar. As a result, they have established a partnership with a local waste management company and a soil additive manufacturer to incorporate the biochar into various soil products.

# 4.2.2. AREAS FOR FUTURE IMPROVEMENT

This section outlines requirements that West Fraser must meet in future audits to meet the requirements of the Puro Biochar Methodology. Requirements for improving the quality of data and accuracy of the LCA and CORC claims in the future include:

This procedure, and other such modifications to improve operations in the future should be noted for review in annual output audits to ensure any impacts on LCA are accounted for.

- Natural Gas Use for Kiln Start-Up is Not Monitored
Issue: The consumption of natural gas for kiln start-up activities is not being monitored, raising concerns about the accuracy of reported emissions. West Fraser currently estimates that natural gas consumption for kiln start-up accounts for 3.9% to 5% of the total yearly plant consumption, currently measured at 128,692 MMBtu (based on estimates in the updated LCA). The emissions from this natural gas consumption were not accounted for in the original LCA submitted to Puro. A revised model accounting for these estimates of natural gas consumption was provided by Accend on 09/17/24.

Proposed Improvement: Natural Gas consumption should be monitored and reported accordingly. Emissions arising from Natural Gas consumption at the facility should be informed by in-situ measurements rather than estimates.

# 4.2.3. VERIFIER RECOMMENDATIONS

This section is not related to the requirements of Puro’s Biochar Methodology. It details suggested measures the CORC supplier could implement to further improve its MRV practices and surpass the requirements set by Puro.

- Insufficient Evidence to Support that Maintenance Emissions do not Meet the Cutoff
Issue: The supplier does not currently provide sufficient numerical evidence demonstrating that plant maintenance emissions are below the chosen LCA cutoff. Estimates of plant operational life were also not provided. Estimates of yearly parts use provided by plant staff support the assumption that the emissions are below the 1% cutoff for the facility’s LCA.

Proposed Improvement: It is recommended that West Fraser monitor all maintenance parts used during upcoming audit periods and include all associated emissions in their LCA.
- Water Use Is Not Monitored on Site
Issue: There is no monitoring of water consumption used for quenching biochar on the project site. Although water input measurement is a requirement of the Puro methodology (section 5.3.2.), water use is expected to be minimal at the site as it uses recycled water from boiler blow off operations for quenching. However, this lack of data could lead to an underestimation of the environmental impact related to water use.

Proposed Improvement: It is recommended that water consumption be monitored in future audits. Given that the water recycling process could not be observed during this audit, monitoring will provide a clearer understanding of the project's water use and potential environmental impact.
